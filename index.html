What the eight weeks look like

Eight weeks of teaching and review, with work most days. After that I stay with you until a company hires you. The sequence is the same either way: code you did not write, one real person’s workflow, public proof, a defense, then the search.

  1. Weeks 1 to 2
    Read and change code you did not write

    A deliberately messy practice codebase lands on your machine. You make it run, trace a ticket through code you did not write, and ship your first change through review. Everything you build after that is yours.

  2. Weeks 3 to 5
    Improve one real person’s workflow

    You pick a problem that passes a written check: a person you can reach, a baseline you measure before building, and a way to tell if it worked. You build it with the AI tooling working engineers use, watch that person use it, and compare. I sign off on each stage in writing.

  3. Weeks 6 to 7
    Predict, rehearse, and make the work findable

    You predict what your changes affect, rehearse the surprise-change exercise, and build a clear project page. Your demo, profile, and resume point to work you can explain. Building a graph tool is an optional extension.

  4. Week 8
    The defense

    Thirty minutes explaining your strongest change with AI closed, then ninety minutes handling a change you did not see coming, with AI open. A working engineer who did not help you scores both on a rubric you saw in advance. Their written verdict is yours to show a hiring manager.

  5. After week 8
    Until you land

    I keep the weekly 1:1, prepare each interview from your own work, and get eyes on the evidence. A company's yes is still theirs. I do not stop because the eight weeks ended.

Every week: a private 45-minute 1:1, a live cohort call, and up to three written reviews on your real work. Plan for 10 to 15 hours a week. Discord in between.

What a hiring manager can check

Rescue

A change to code you inherited

A pull request, review discussion, failure log, and check that catches the defect. Explain what you changed and why.

Improve

A workflow someone actually uses

A named problem, a baseline, a running project, and observations afterward. State the sample and what the numbers cannot establish.

Defend

Your explanation and an unexpected change

A recording with AI closed, an exercise with AI available, and an independent reader’s written assessment.

Share

A page that makes the evidence easy to find

Your contribution, a short demo, the checks, and the limits. Profile, resume, and interview practice use the same work.

How I work with you

Bootcamps have a curriculum and a cohort. You get a senior engineer who reads your code every week for eight weeks, then until you land, and a room of five who notice when you go quiet.

  • Weekly 1:1, 45 minutes. Your code, your plan, your blockers. Same slot every week.
  • Up to three written reviews a week. On real work. Accepted, or sent back with the one thing to change first.
  • Sign-offs on every project. Nothing counts as done because you said so.
  • A live cohort call. Up to five people, 75 minutes, what shipped and what broke.
  • The defense. An outside engineer, on camera, filling in the form on the right.
  • Discord, daily. Stuck for thirty honest minutes, you ask. I answer the same day.

Straight answers

Will you get me a job?

I make you the candidate a team can evaluate and trust: real ownership on live systems, written review, a public site, an outside engineer's verdict, and my own reference. I prepare every interview from your own work and I keep working with you until you land. A company's yes is still theirs.

Why eight weeks?

That is the intensive teaching period. The current workload estimate is close to 15 hours a week; with 10 hours, completing the artifacts may take longer. Short programs end there. I do not. If you are not hired at week 8 I keep the weekly 1:1, the interview prep, and getting eyes on the work, until a company hires you. Eight weeks is the program. It is not a hire-by date.

Is this a bootcamp?

No. Bootcamps teach beginners a stack. You already write programs. This is a residency: work on real systems with a senior engineer, sign-offs, and proof, for someone who already has the degree or the equivalent.

What if I'm slower or faster than the timeline?

The timeline is a sequence, not a clock. The map opens the next step when you finish the one before it. Work every day and the pace is yours.

I never got hired after the AI wave. Is that me?

If you can't write a working program yet, learn that first; I'll point you at free material. If you can, the missing piece is almost never another tutorial. It is proof someone else can trust, and the craft of using AI in a way you can defend.

Why founding, with no placements yet?

Because the first five get me at full attention while the method is tuned on them, at the lowest rate anyone will pay. Week 2 is the exit. Once they have defended in front of outside engineers, the rate rises and the room gets harder to get into.
