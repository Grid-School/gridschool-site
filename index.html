What the year looks like

Not a cohort clock. A sequence you move through at your own pace, on the condition that you put work in every day. Most people take two to four months to reach the defense; the rest of the year is where the position comes from.

  1. Months 1 to 2
    Read and change a live system

    A multiplayer world with real users lands on your machine. You make it run, trace a ticket through code you did not write, then ship your first change through review and watch it deploy.

  2. Months 2 to 4
    Your own product, shipped

    You model a system, build it with the AI tooling working engineers use, put it in front of real users, and measure the outcome. I sign off on each stage in writing.

  3. Months 3 to 6
    Graph tool, design system, public site

    You build a tool that reads source code as a graph and use it to plan your own changes. We agree a design system in a 1:1 and you build your site in it, one page per project, and start publishing.

  4. Month 4 onward
    The defense

    Eight recorded minutes defending your strongest change to a working engineer who did not help you. Their written verdict is yours to show a hiring manager.

  5. Until you land
    Interviews, and keeping up

    The tech moves every quarter. We keep your projects current, absorb what's new, prepare for each interview from your own work, and adjust the search. I stay reachable after you are hired.

Every week: a private 45-minute 1:1, a live cohort call, and up to three written reviews on your real work. Plan for 10 to 15 hours a week. Discord in between.

How I work with you

Bootcamps have a curriculum and a cohort. You get a senior engineer who reads your code every week, for a year, and a room of five who notice when you go quiet.

  • Weekly 1:1, 45 minutes. Your code, your plan, your blockers. Same slot every week.
  • Three written reviews a week. On real work. Accepted, or sent back with the one thing to change first.
  • Sign-offs on every project. Nothing counts as done because you said so.
  • A live cohort call. Five people, one hour, what shipped and what broke.
  • The defense. An outside engineer, on camera, filling in the form on the right.
  • Discord, daily. Stuck for thirty honest minutes, you ask. I answer the same day.

Straight answers

Will you get me a job?

I make you the candidate a team can evaluate and trust: real ownership on live systems, written review, a public site, an outside engineer's verdict, and my own reference. We prepare every interview from your own work and I keep working with you until you land. A company's yes is still theirs.

Why 12 months?

Because that is how long it actually takes to go from "I can code" to "a team hired me" in this market, and because the tech changes while you search. Short programs hand you a certificate and leave. Here the projects stay current and the mentor stays.

Is this a bootcamp?

No. Bootcamps teach beginners a stack. You already write programs. This is a residency: work on real systems with a senior engineer, sign-offs, and proof, for someone who already has the degree or the equivalent.

What if I'm slower or faster than the timeline?

The timeline is a sequence, not a clock. The map opens the next step when you finish the one before it. Work every day and the pace is yours.

I never got hired after the AI wave. Is that me?

If you can't write a working program yet, learn that first; I'll point you at free material. If you can, the missing piece is almost never another tutorial. It is proof someone else can trust, and the craft of using AI in a way you can defend.

Why founding, with no placements yet?

Because the first five get me at full attention while the method is tuned on them, at the lowest rate anyone will pay. Week 2 is the exit. Once they have defended in front of outside engineers, the rate rises and the room gets harder to get into.
