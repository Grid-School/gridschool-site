    Who you work with

    I'm Aden. I build applied AI systems for engineering teams.

    My work is applied AI and agentic graph engineering: systems that map how a codebase fits together, and agents that make changes a team can check before it trusts them. These are problems engineering teams have right now, and they are the problems your map is aimed at.

    I learned to build before the AI era, writing systems from scratch and shipping software people depended on before LLMs arrived. When the wave hit, it was clear engineers would need help. The tools changed faster than anyone could explain them, and it got harder for a team to tell who was good.

    So this is where my time goes. I automate the busywork of a search with the same kind of systems I build, and I plan a map for each engineer around their own goal, so no week goes to work that does not move you toward the role you want.

    Straight answers

    Will you get me a job?

    Nobody honest can promise that. What we do: roles that fit you every morning, notes to real people, a real-user project a hiring manager can check, and every reply tracked. A company's yes is theirs.

    I already have years of experience. Is this too junior for me?

    Your map starts where you are. For working engineers it usually goes straight to turning past work into proof a stranger can check, the titles that fit now, and the rounds where you stall.

    How is the map decided?

    The sentence at the top of this page drafts it from three answers, using the same steps the platform runs. The free check finishes it from your resume and where your search stops. Every step ends in something a person can check.

    Why not follow the map on my own?

    You can, and the map is yours to keep. What you'd be doing by hand: researching roles and the person to reach every morning, drafting each note, tracking every reply and follow-up, and finding a real user for your project. In the cohort you also get clinics and people at the same stage.

    Is this a bootcamp?

    No. A bootcamp teaches a stack to beginners. This starts from software you can already write and works on what gets you hired: a ready resume, sends to real people, and one project for a real user.

    Why trust you, with no placements yet?

    Don't take it on trust. The free check runs on your own situation before you pay anything, the sprint is free, and we publish the sprint's numbers at weeks 3 and 6, the weak weeks included.
