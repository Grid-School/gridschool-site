# Respond to an unexpected change

The Arena is a 90-minute exercise on a copy of a system you have worked on. Your reviewer changes one condition, such as an unavailable API, a repeated request, or a lower spending limit. You decide what to change, use AI if helpful, and show what your checks establish.

You rehearse in week 7. A second version accompanies your final defense. The defense also has a separate 30-minute explanation with AI closed. Calendar lists both sessions so you can plan the time. The reviewer shares the assessment criteria before either attempt.

| Time | What you do |
|---|---|
| First 10 minutes | Restate the changed requirement, identify the risk, and predict affected code before prompting |
| Next 15 minutes | Reproduce the failure or establish a baseline; choose a small plan and spending limit |
| Next 35 minutes | Implement or contain the problem using AI, documentation, and tests |
| Next 20 minutes | Try an adversarial case, check the original behavior, and show recovery |
| Final 10 minutes | Explain the evidence, uncertainty, cost, and next decision |

A justified decision to stop an unsafe action is a valid result. Passing requires evidence for the behavior you claim; a working-looking screen alone is insufficient. If a tool fails, save the failure, explain the fallback, and agree on a fair retry. Accessibility adjustments and extra reading time can be arranged with Aden before the session.

Save the changed requirement, your initial prediction, the code difference, checks, AI work record, and a short explanation. The reviewer scores comprehension, verification, communication, and judgment about what to do next from 0 to 2. A 0 means you have not demonstrated the ability; a 1 means you need a cue or have a material gap; a 2 means you demonstrate it independently with relevant evidence. Both parts require a 2 in every dimension. Before rehearsal, ask Aden to walk through the detailed rubric with you. A requested change comes with a specific practice task and a new variant for the reassessment. Ask everyone on a recording before publishing it.

**Done when** you have received the written result for the exercise and can explain the limits of what you demonstrated.
