Straight answers

Who is it for?

Someone 18 or older who can already write software and wants an engineering role: in their final year of a computer science degree, a recent graduate who hasn't found a position yet, or someone who needs a project that makes a team stop and look. The map is built for English-language engineering roles: the US, Canada, the UK, Singapore, Australia, and remote teams. They can study or live anywhere.

Can they start before they graduate?

Yes, and the final year is a good time. Many new-graduate roles open close to a year before the start date, and a project built now is ready when they apply.

What will I see?

If they agree: a short weekly email with the steps they finished, how many notes and applications they sent, replies, and what's next. You won't see what we talk about on calls, their drafts, or my reviews of their work. If they'd rather keep it private, you'll get one email saying they accepted.

What if they don't want it?

They say so from the email, and you're refunded in full. It works far better as their choice than as an assignment, so I'd rather they decline than start reluctantly.

Is a job guaranteed?

No, and be wary of anyone who says it is. A company's yes is theirs. What I promise is the work: their map, the course, my review of what they send, and in the Residency, me building with them every week.

Who are you?

Aden Jessee. I'm a working engineer: I build applied AI and agentic systems now, and I built software for years before that. GridSchool is the system I built to plan and run a job search the way I'd run an engineering project.
